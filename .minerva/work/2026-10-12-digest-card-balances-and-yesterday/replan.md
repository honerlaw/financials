# Replan log: digest-card-balances-and-yesterday

## 2026-10-12 — Key the Yesterday section on the purchase date, not the posting date

**Original plan**: Approach step 1 selected the Yesterday section's rows by `Transaction.date == yesterday` — the budget line's own date key — and claimed that including pending rows never counts a purchase twice.

**What changed**: Review finding #2 (independent code review). For a *posted* transaction Plaid's `date` is the posting date; the purchase date is `authorized_date` (stored at `app/sync.py:432`, nullable). A purchase reported while pending under its purchase day was reported again, under its posting day, in a later digest once it posted — sync flags the pending row `removed` and inserts the posted row with a later `date` — and a purchase with no pending stage was labelled with the day it posted. "Never counted twice" held within one digest only: a wrong core assumption.

**New plan**: The Yesterday section selects rows by one helper, `spending.purchase_date(txn)` = `authorized_date or date` (read with `getattr`, so a transaction-like object without the field falls back to `date`); `day_spend` re-filters on it. `is_spend`, the `removed` filter and the single transaction fetch are unchanged, and the budget line and four-week history stay keyed on `date` — knowledge 022 decision 3 (one spend definition, one query) is **not** superseded; only the Yesterday date key differs, and the promote-time knowledge entry cross-links 022.

- **Fetch window.** Assuming Plaid's `authorized_date <= date` (observed behaviour, not a documented guarantee), a row purchased yesterday has `date` in `[yesterday, today]`, inside `[week_start(today) - 4 weeks, week_start(today) + 7 days)`. If the assumption were ever violated the row could only be missed when `date` predates the four-week window — negligible.
- **Invariant, restated.** Every Yesterday row is still counted in some printed week total (its `date` is yesterday or today). What changes is that the row can be counted in a different week bucket than its Yesterday label implies: on a Sunday digest, a Saturday purchase that posted Sunday sits under "Yesterday — Sat" but in this week's budget line, not last week's history.
- **Residuals, accepted** (frequency per issuer — AmEx, Citi, Truist — unknown; production DB is firewalled from the workstation):
  (a) a posted row with no `authorized_date` falls back to `date` and can still be reported a second time under its posting day;
  (b) Plaid returning different `authorized_date` values for the pending and posted versions of one purchase can do the same;
  (c) a purchase first synced after its purchase day has passed (no pending stage, e.g. purchased Sunday, first seen Tuesday) is never shown in any Yesterday block — this generalises the proposal's existing late-arrival bullet. Omission is judged the lesser failure: the purchase still counts in the week totals, whereas a duplicate puts a wrong figure in a Yesterday total.
  "Never counted twice" becomes "never shown twice when Plaid supplies the same `authorized_date` on the pending and posted rows". A per-transaction "already reported" record, or dedupe via `pending_transaction_id`, would close (a) and (b) but adds digest-side state for a rare case — out of scope.
- **Superseded proposal passages** — `replan.md` governs on any conflict — edited in the same change: Approach step 1 (the `Transaction.date == yesterday` key and the "never counted twice" claim), success criterion 3 ("dated yesterday" → "purchased yesterday — `authorized_date`, else `date`"), and the Open Questions late-arrival bullet (replaced by residuals a–c).
- **Tests.** Criterion 3 gains a three-case pending→posted test: a pending row and its posted replacement with `authorized_date` set yield one Yesterday entry across the two digests; a posted row with null `authorized_date` falls back to `date` (documents residual a); a removed pending row is excluded. `day_spend` and `_spend_totals` docstrings are rewritten to match.
- **Follow-up, not blocking.** How often each issuer omits or changes `authorized_date` (residuals a, b) is unmeasured; observable from the app over HTTP, recorded as a standing fact at promote.
